# E-Dialect Organization Profile Manifesto Design

## Positioning

The organization profile should present E-Dialect as an open engineering
community for reusable local-language digital infrastructure. It must not
rename the organization to 乡音开源计划 or imply that one current initiative,
one company, or one language application is the whole community. 乡声万语 is
shown as the current flagship initiative and as one example of the initiatives
that E-Dialect can host.

## Information architecture

The page opens with a compact manifesto: the recurring problem is duplicated,
one-off digitization work, and the proposed answer is reusable software,
standards, and auditable collaboration. A short principle separates common
technical infrastructure from language knowledge owned and curated by local
communities.

The ecosystem is described as a flow rather than a repository catalogue:
万语校坊 turns source material into reviewed structured data; 乡声集盒 supports
public use and evidence-backed contribution; 兴化语记 is the flagship Puxian
language application feeding real requirements back into the common stack.
Semantic retrieval, IPA-to-speech, voice processing, and related work are
labelled experimental capabilities rather than bundled into a fictional mature
万语引擎 product.

## Trust and calls to action

Developers, language researchers, and local contributors receive distinct,
concrete participation paths. Links point only to live repositories, the
public Project #7 board, the official website, and merged governance documents.
The footer keeps a concise legal boundary: repository-specific licensing,
separate data/recording/model permissions, and the distinction among the open
community, current initiatives, and Beijing Taju's limited commercial/legal
role.

No MIT badge, empty link, unverified corpus count, exaggerated national-scale
claim, or “demo” framing is used. Verification checks link targets, terminology,
relative governance links, and the unchanged license/data boundary.
